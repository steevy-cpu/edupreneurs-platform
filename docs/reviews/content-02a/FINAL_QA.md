The sequence moves from digit value to operations, then exact division, factors and divisibility. School supplies, public events, measures and grouping give learners concrete entry points. The strongest feedback shows how to check a result independently. Several weaker items merely restate the calculation. A learner choosing B throughout earns **16/30** in either static choice order. A is correct six times, C eight and D never. Redistribute answer positions, then try a short mixed set with plausible distractors and one task where the learner explains a choice. Recognition scores alone provide little evidence for objectives such as setting up columns or producing a number in words.

For a narrow phone, L1's French place-value paragraph is 301 characters, L5 lists seven worked steps for the factors of 12, and L6 pairs long choice text with mathematical symbols. Have a learner read and navigate an item at 320 dp and large text in each language. Check whether the sign, grouped number and unit stay intelligible across line breaks, the final choice and feedback are reachable, and a reader can revisit the example. Reduce the L5 list using factor pairs and split regrouping into labelled steps. Layout, audio pronunciation and screen-reader order remain unmeasured.
